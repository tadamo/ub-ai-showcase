CONCEPT

A colorful top-down lane-crossing arcade game, with original artwork and code. The player is a UB student late for class who has to get from the Flint Loop bus stop on North Campus to the lecture hall door, dodging Stampede buses, cars circling for parking, bikes, other students on e-scooters, Canada geese that refuse to yield, geese that swoop in low and bomb the path, and the goose poop that covers every patch of grass. Each board cleared makes the commute a little faster and meaner. The campus goose population is a running student joke; lean into it. The tone is dry, friendly campus humor: the kind of jokes students would tell about their own commute. Keep it good-natured. No jokes at the expense of real people, departments, or groups.

GAMEPLAY

- The player moves one square per press: Arrow keys or WASD, a swipe on the play area, or a visible on-screen D-pad. One move per press; ignore key-repeat. Moves are quick (around 120 milliseconds) and can't be queued more than one move ahead.
- Hazards move in lanes at fixed speeds and directions, wrapping off-screen. Each lane has its own speed and spacing. Buses are slow and long, cars are medium, bikes and students on e-scooters are fast and short. Mix e-scooter riders into road lanes from level 1, and add more of them at higher levels. Geese wander slowly along lawn rows and pause at random.
- Goose poop sits still on lawn and sidewalk squares. Place a few piles at the start of each crossing, more at higher levels. Geese occasionally leave a fresh pile behind as they waddle, up to a per-level cap. Stepping in poop costs a life, like any other hazard. Poop clears at the start of each new crossing.
- Flying geese start at level 2. A goose swoops in low from one side and flies straight across a single row (any row except start and goal), over roads and lawns alike. Warn first: show a flashing arrow and the goose's shadow at the screen edge on that row about 1 second before it enters. Touching a flying goose costs a life.
- Flying geese drop poop. While crossing, a goose can drop one or two droppings onto squares below its path or the rows next to it. Show a target ring on the landing square about 0.8 seconds before impact, shrinking as the dropping falls. If the player is on that square at impact, they lose a life. A dropping that lands on a lawn or sidewalk square stays there as regular ground poop. One that lands on a road splats and fades within a second.
- Touching any hazard costs a life. Start with 3 lives, shown as coffee cups. After a hit, freeze briefly, show a message for that hazard, and return the player to the start of the current crossing. Keep the level and timer running.
- Each crossing has a countdown labeled "Class starts in". Start at 30 seconds. If it hits zero, lose a life and restart the crossing with a fresh timer.
- Reaching the goal row completes the crossing. Show a short success line, then start the next level.
- Difficulty ramps gradually with each board cleared, never in big jumps. Each new level raises every lane's speed by a small step (about 8 percent), up to a playable cap. Traffic gets a little denser, more e-scooter riders appear, flying geese come more often, drop more poop, and fly a bit faster, and there's more starting ground poop. Each of these has its own cap. Within a level, speeds stay constant so players can read the lanes. Restarting returns to level 1 speeds.
- Give each level a name shown at its start:
  1. "Syllabus Week"
  2. "First Quiz"
  3. "Midterms"
  4. "Lake Effect Advisory" (light snowfall overlay, visuals only)
  5. "Finals Week"
  After level 5, keep cycling with "Finals Week (again)" and increasing difficulty to the cap.
- Score: +10 for each new row reached in a crossing (no points for revisiting rows), +100 for reaching class, and +5 for each second left on the timer. Retain the best score on this browser with localStorage. If storage is unavailable, keep an in-memory best score and let play continue.
- Game over when lives reach zero. Show score, best score, levels cleared, a closing line, and Play Again.

FAIRNESS

- Every lane must always have gaps the player can step into. Derive minimum gap size from lane speed and move duration so a player who waits for an opening can always cross.
- Geese and poop together must never fully block a lawn row; leave at least two open squares at all times. Never drop poop on the start row, the goal row, the player's current square, or the square they're moving into. Keep a clear path from start to goal at all times.
- Use slightly forgiving hitboxes inside the visible artwork. A near miss should look like a near miss.
- Flying geese and falling poop must always be dodgeable: the warning and target ring must give enough time to step one square away, never target more than one square in the player's 3-by-3 neighborhood at once, and never land on the start or goal row. Only one flying goose on screen at a time, and no flyovers during the first 3 seconds of a crossing or a respawn grace period.
- Give a 1-second grace period after each respawn where the player blinks and can't be hit, and keep the start row hazard-free.
- Keep level 1 easy enough that a first-time visitor usually reaches class on the first or second try.
